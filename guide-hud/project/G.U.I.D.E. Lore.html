02 Backstory

Ros Chrisstone was born in Castella, a grey fishing town on the western reach of Brettany, where the harbour bells kept better time than any clock and the salt got into everything — the bread, the ledgers, the lungs of old men. The Chrisstones mended nets and sailed when the weather allowed, and Ros grew up expecting the sea to be the whole of a life.

It was the levy that took them inland. When Brettany called its coastal towns to muster, Castella sent its sons by the cartload, and Ros went among them — broad in the shoulder before they were grown, and steadier under a sergeant's voice than most twice their age. The drill yard suited them in a way the boats never had. They learned the long blade, then learned to carry two, and the garrison stopped laughing about it by the second winter.

Eleven years under the Crown's banner wore the coast out of their accent and put a soldier's plainness in its place. Ros served garrison rotations from the Brettany wall to the Davelguay marches, made few friends and fewer enemies, and earned a reputation for being exactly where they were posted and nothing more — which, in a faltering kingdom, was worth more than cleverness.

The Reclamation found them between commissions, with a debt to settle and no lord left to settle it to. Ros took the assignment for the coin and the work, and followed the road back toward Castella — toward a stolen tome, a magistrate's writ, and a woman who would not give her name.
